# Black boxes aren't black
- Question: how does a flight recorder survive the crash it records — and why is it orange?

## Facts
1. Recorders are orange so searchers can spot them in wreckage — AP/NTSB, https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/plane-black-boxes-plane-crash-investigations/6131009/
2. Usually installed in the tail, the most survivable section — same AP/NTSB source.
3. Crash-survivable memory unit: 3,400 g impact; 1,100 °C fire for 1 hour; deep-sea pressure 20,000 ft — EUROCAE ED-112A via TSB Canada, https://bst-tsb.gc.ca/eng/medias-media/fiches-facts/a20f0002/enregistreurs-recorders/20200309.html and ANSV https://ansv.it/en/flight-recorders/
4. Underwater locator beacon activates in water and transmits for 90 days (older units: 30 days) — TSB Canada (above); pings once per second at 37.5 kHz.
5. Fighter pilots lose consciousness around 9 g even with a g-suit (G-LOC) — general aviation-medicine figure; phrased loosely ("blacks out at 9").
