## Facts
1. Flip Flap Railway, Sea Lion Park, Coney Island, 1895: completely circular loop (~25 ft); repeated complaints of neck and back strains/whiplash — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip_Flap_Railway (g figures conflict: 6–8 / 12 / 14 g → not used)
2. Circular loop, frictionless: minimum speed at the top v² = g·r; energy over the 2r drop → v_bottom² = 5 g·r; felt load at bottom = 1 + v²/(g·r) = 6 g, independent of r — computed (Newton + energy conservation); cf. https://ffden-2.phys.uaf.edu/211_fall2002.web.dir/shawna_sastamoinen/Clothoid_Loop.htm
3. 6 g ⇒ you feel six times your body weight — definition of load factor
4. Clothoid/teardrop: larger radius at the bottom (fast → gentle turn), smaller at the top (slow → still enough push) — UAF page above; https://mycentennial.sd43.bc.ca/gradys2021/2025/04/08/the-clothoid-loop-physics-12
5. The (Great American) Revolution, Six Flags Magic Mountain, opened 8 May 1976 — first modern coaster with a vertical loop; designer Werner Stengel; non-circular clothoid loop; 4.9 g listed — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_American_Revolution ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Stengel → said as "under 5 g"
