# HOOK
Your friend tried to build a backyard pool for his giant pocket puh, and accidentally flooded an entire desert canyon.
So you build a concrete wall massive enough to stop the river.

# DIVERSION TUNNELS
First problem.
You can't pour wet concrete underwater while a raging river sweeps your workers away.
So you dynamite four giant tunnels, 17 meters wide, straight through solid canyon rock.
You plug the river and reroute every drop right around your construction site.

# ARCH WEDGE
Next, you build the wall 221 meters tall.
But a flat concrete wall would snap like a cracker under millions of tons of water.
So you curve it into a giant arch, 200 meters thick at the bottom and 14 meters wide at the top.
The harder the river pushes, the tighter it wedges the arch straight into the canyon cliffs.

# COOLING PIPES
Wait. Huge problem.
Pouring three million cubic yards of concrete in one lump traps insane chemical heat.
It would take 125 years to cool down, and shatter into dust.
So you pour it like interlocking Lego blocks, laced with 930 kilometers of steel pipes.
You pump ice cold water from a giant refrigeration plant through every pipe, then inject cement grout to fuse it solid.

# TURBINES & PAYOFF
Finally, you drop 17 giant turbines at the base, turning river pressure into 2,000 megawatts of power for Las Vegas.
Congratulations.
You've plugged a raging canyon with ice cooled concrete, just to fix your friend's pool.
You call it, the Hoover Dam.
Follow, and next time, I'll save your pocket puh.
