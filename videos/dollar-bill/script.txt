# HOOK
Your friend wants to buy your used pocket puh.
You say, 100 dollars.
He's broke, so he prints one at home.
Now you have to build a bill he can't fake.

# PAPER
Paper turns to mush in the wash.
So you use 75 percent cotton, 25 percent linen.
Basically, jeans.
4,000 double folds to tear.

# THE PEN
Cashiers test it with an iodine pen.
Fake paper turns black. Cotton stays yellow.
So your friend bleaches a real 5 dollar bill, and prints 100 on top.
It passes.

# UV LIGHT
So you hide a thread inside, and a watermark.
Under UV light, a real 100 glows pink.
His bleached 5 glows blue.
Busted.

# PHOTOSHOP
So he scans one into Photoshop.
Blocked.
Real bills hide secret dots, and digital code.
Copiers and Photoshop refuse to copy them.
Infinite money glitch, patched.

# MICROTECH
So he buys a real press.
You add raised ink, color shifting ink, and microprinting on the collar.
Ours says, The United States of Pocket Puh.
Ben Franklin even hid typos on early money.
Fakers fixed them, and caught themselves.

# 3D RIBBON
Then you weave in a blue 3D ribbon, packed with hundreds of thousands of lenses.
Tilt it, and the bells turn into hundreds.

# SECRET SERVICE
He still won't stop.
So you start a secret police force, just for fake money.
Sounds like a joke. It isn't.
In 1865, a third of US cash was fake.
Lincoln approved the Secret Service the day he was shot.
They didn't guard presidents until 1901.

# SCALE
Costs 11 cents to print. Lasts 24 years.
And 80 percent live outside America.

# PAYOFF
He finally borrows a real one from grandma.
Congratulations. You've built cotton nobody can copy, with its own police force.
You call it, the hundred dollar bill.

# FOLLOW
Follow, or I'll report your pocket puh to the Secret Service.
