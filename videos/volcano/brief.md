# The scariest volcanoes have the slowest lava
- Question: why does one volcano gently pour lava while another blows itself apart?
- Length: ~38 s · Language: en · Voice: chatterbox default, block mode, pitch −1 · Music: tension
- Palette: deep (night) + one daylight set · Captions: punch
- Audience: everyone who pictures volcanoes as rivers of lava
- Protagonist: the gas bubble — trapped vs escaping; two volcanoes compared
- Loop: "…So the danger isn't the lava you can watch. Which is why…" → "The scariest volcanoes have the slowest lava."
- Tone: 57 people died at St. Helens — no people in danger on screen; one hiker only in the safe Hawaii beat.

## Facts
1. Kīlauea 2018 lava ≈ 2,070–2,085 °F ≈ 1,130–1,140 °C ("about 1,100 degrees") — https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/lava-temperatures-were-about-2000-degrees-fahrenheit
2. A 2018 Kīlauea flow front advanced 40–60 yards/hour (≈ 37–55 m/h → "about 50 meters an hour"; flows vary, some much faster) — https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/observatory-messages?page=70
3. Silica-rich (sticky) magma makes it hard for dissolved gas to escape → pressure builds → explosive; low-viscosity basalt lets gas escape → effusive — https://volcanoes.usgs.gov/vsc/glossary/silica.html ; https://volcanoes.sdsu.edu/Controls.html ; https://www.nps.gov/subjects/volcanoes/volcanic-eruptions.htm
4. 18 May 1980: the north flank gave way in a debris avalanche, the sudden pressure drop released the lateral blast — https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/msh/lateral.html
5. Trees flattened up to 19 miles (≈ 30 km) away; ~230 sq mi devastated — same USGS page
6. Lost ≈ 1,300 ft (396 m) of height — https://usgs.gov/faqs/how-high-was-mount-st-helens-may-18-1980-eruption-how-high-was-it-after
7. Eiffel Tower ≈ 330 m incl. antennas → 396 m > 330 m (computed)
